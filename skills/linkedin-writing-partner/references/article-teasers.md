# Article Teasers

Use this mode when the LinkedIn post points to a longer article,
case study, project write-up, or technical essay.

The post must not become a compressed copy of the article.

## Post-to-article connection

The post and article must form one continuous reading path. The post should
raise a concrete question, tension, or promise that the article directly
answers, supports, or develops. Sharing a topic is not enough.

Define this connection before drafting and carry it through the workflow:

- **Source:** identify the specific article material that provides the payoff.
- **Interview:** ask only for missing context needed to establish the opening,
  payoff, or intended link placement.
- **Angles:** reject an angle when the article cannot substantively continue it.
- **Thesis:** distinguish the point made by the post from what the article adds.
- **Narrative:** lead naturally to the boundary between those two ideas.
- **Draft:** make the transition concrete; a generic “read more” is not enough.
- **Review:** read the post followed immediately by the article and check that
  the second continues the exact thread opened by the first.

Use the supplied article URL, or clearly mark the user's intended link location
when it belongs elsewhere. Never invent a URL. If the article content or a
reliable summary is unavailable, ask for it before claiming that the connection
works.

Its job is to communicate:

1. enough context to understand the situation
2. one concrete incident or observation
3. the interesting tension it revealed
4. why the full story is worth reading

Default target: 120–180 words.

Reveal at most one major insight.

Do not include the complete:

- incident
- diagnosis
- implementation
- architecture
- trade-offs
- conclusion

Leave at least one meaningful technical question unresolved in the post, and
verify that the article resolves or materially develops it.

## Final test

Ask all three:

- Could someone read this post instead of the article and understand most of
  its technical reasoning? If yes, reveal less.
- Does the article deliver the specific payoff promised by the post? If not,
  change the angle or the promise.
- Does moving from the final line to the article feel like the natural next
  step? If not, strengthen the transition without resorting to engagement bait.
